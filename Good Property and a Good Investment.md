# Understanding the Difference Between a Good Property and a Good Investment

A property that looks good isn't always a good investment. A property might look nice, but location, demand, price, and potential matter more.

Firstly, investors need to clearly identify their purpose for purchasing. For rental returns and the accumulation of long-term asset growth. Different properties are suited to different purposes.

Location is one of the most crucial factors to examine. Such services and facilities, such as the proximity to roads, schools, workplaces and public transport, everyday needs and services, affect demand and future value.

Equally, price research is essential. By comparing it with comparable properties within the local area, potential buyers are best able to make a judgment on whether it's reasonable or just being priced by speculative market fever.

According to [Rakesh Israni](https://e27.co/user/rakesh.israni/), buyers must see beyond the facade of the apartment. Smart design is useful to a certain extent, but a property must have a strategic location, practical need and solid financial reason, he thinks.

Your investment may also be impacted by future development. An area's investment appeal can increase due to new roads, business parks and facilities, public and transport infrastructure, yet the buyer should confirm the plans before going ahead with the investment.

Rental potential is another important point. Investors should study actual rental demand and compare expected income with maintenance, taxes, loan costs and other ownership expenses.

Always consider the legal check. Before entering into a large commitment, look at title deed records, authorisations, agreements and relevant property documents.

It enables investors to ask smarter questions, avoiding emotionally-led decisions. Israni underlines research, patience and appropriate valuation when judging a property.

So in other words, a 'good property' is an investment that everyone will want, while a 'good investment' is an investment which also makes sense financially for the buyer.
